# 13–22. PAGE SPECIFICATIONS

## Page 1 — Homepage (/)

The homepage should establish credibility and guide visitors towards the next useful action. It must not attempt to explain the entire company in one enormous page.

### Section A: Main navigation

Create a restrained navigation bar containing:
- SIWI Global Ventures logo.
- Home.
- About Us.
- Products.
- Trade Enquiries.
- Contact.
- Dark/light theme toggle.
- A visually prominent but restrained enquiry CTA.

The Products navigation item should provide access to the product categories. Use a dropdown or accessible mega-menu only if it genuinely improves navigation. Keep the menu compact.

On scroll, the navigation may become sticky, provided it does not consume too much vertical space. Use a subtle background transition rather than a large shadow.

### Section B: Hero

Use the art direction described in section 8. The hero should communicate the company's identity and core business without excessive explanatory text.

### Section C: Product focus

Introduce the core product categories.

- **Suggested heading:** Products for global supply requirements.
- **Supporting copy:** "Explore our range of dehydrated vegetable products and other traded goods."

Present the product categories through an editorial composition. Do not create six identical cards. Make the dehydrated vegetable range the primary visual focus. Keep Tiles and Other Products available through a secondary navigation path.

### Section D: How SIWI works

Create a concise explanation of the company's business approach. Use three clear steps:

- **01 — Understand the requirement:** Understand the buyer's product and sourcing requirements.
- **02 — Discuss the supply:** Discuss the relevant product range, specifications, and commercial requirements.
- **03 — Build the partnership:** Work towards dependable, long-term business relationships.

Present these as a numbered sequence or a horizontal editorial layout. Do not imply that these steps represent a formally certified or established operating procedure unless confirmed by the company.

### Section E: About preview

Use a two-column editorial layout. One side should contain a relevant visual or the compass motif. The other should contain a short company introduction.

- **Suggested heading:** Rooted in India. Focused on global trade.
- **CTA:** Discover SIWI Global Ventures

### Section F: Trade enquiries

Create a visually distinctive enquiry section.

- **Suggested heading:** Looking for a dependable product sourcing partner?
- **Supporting copy:** "Tell us what you are looking for. We will use your enquiry to understand your requirements and discuss the next steps."
- **CTA:** Start a Trade Enquiry

### Section G: Footer

Include:
- Logo.
- Tagline.
- Primary navigation.
- Product category links.
- Phone.
- Email.
- Mumbai, India.
- Privacy Policy.
- Terms.
- Copyright notice.

Do not add fabricated social media profiles. Only display social links if the company provides the actual URLs.

## Page 2 — About Us (/about)

This page should feel editorial and human. Do not turn the company introduction into a collection of generic corporate cards.

### Hero

- **Eyebrow:** ABOUT SIWI GLOBAL VENTURES
- **Headline:** Connecting Indian products with global opportunity.
- **Supporting paragraph:** "SIWI Global Ventures is an India-based import-export company focused on dependable trade partnerships, quality products, competitive pricing, and service that puts business relationships first."

### Company introduction

Use the supplied company description as the source. Break it into readable paragraphs. Do not place the entire company introduction inside a large rounded card.

### Our focus

Explain the two broad areas of business:

- **Dehydrated vegetable products:** Introduce the onion and garlic product range, alongside other dehydrated and value-added products.
- **Other traded products:** Explain that SIWI also deals in tiles and other products according to market requirements.

Keep the primary focus on the dehydrated vegetable export business.

### Our approach

Use three concise principles:
- Quality-focused product selection.
- Dependable communication and service.
- Long-term international trade relationships.

These should be presented as the company's stated priorities, not as independently verified performance claims.

### Founder

Introduce Harshvardhan Patil.

- **Suggested heading:** A business built around trust.
- **Supporting copy:** "Founder Harshvardhan Patil leads SIWI Global Ventures with a focus on connecting Indian products with international markets and developing dependable business relationships."

Do not invent a founder biography, qualifications, career history, or personal achievements. Use a portrait only if an authentic image is supplied. Otherwise, use typography and the compass graphic.

### Closing CTA

- **Text:** Let's discuss your sourcing requirements.
- **Button:** Contact SIWI

## Page 3 — Products (/products)

This is the main product catalogue. It must be designed for B2B discovery rather than consumer shopping.

### Hero

- **Eyebrow:** OUR PRODUCTS
- **Headline:** A considered range of products for global trade.
- **Supporting copy:** "Explore our dehydrated vegetable products and other traded categories. For product specifications, availability, and commercial requirements, contact our team."

### Product category structure

**01. Dehydrated Vegetable Products** — Give this category the strongest visual prominence.

Include:
- Onion Powder.
- Garlic Powder.
- Garlic Granules.
- Dehydrated Fried Onions.
- Birista.
- Onion Kibbles.
- Other Value-Added Products.

Use genuine product photography wherever possible. Each product should link to its corresponding detail page.

**02. Tiles** — Present the secondary trading category separately. Do not allow it to visually compete with the primary dehydrated vegetable range. Link to /products/tiles.

**03. Other Products** — Create a flexible category that can accommodate additional verified products in the future. Link to /products/other-products.

### Product navigation

Provide simple category filters or anchor navigation if they improve usability. Avoid unnecessary filtering interfaces, especially when the catalogue is small. Do not create a complex search dashboard for a handful of products.

### Enquiry interaction

Every product should provide a clear route to enquiry. When a visitor selects a product enquiry CTA, preselect that product in the enquiry form. The visitor should not need to enter the product name manually again.

## Page 4 — Product Detail Template (individual product routes)

Every product page should feel specific to the product while sharing a consistent design framework.

### Hero composition

Use a product image and an editorial text column. For example, the Onion Powder page should show onion powder rather than an unrelated photograph.

Include:
- Breadcrumb navigation.
- Product name.
- Short description.
- Relevant product image.
- Enquiry CTA.

### Product information

Include only verified information.

Possible sections:
- **Product overview:** Explain the product clearly.
- **Product formats:** Describe the available form, grade, or particle size only when confirmed.
- **Applications:** Mention suitable applications only when supported by the company's actual product information.
- **Specifications:** Show verified specifications when available. If not available: "Contact our team to discuss product specifications and availability."
- **Packaging:** Show available packaging options only after confirmation.
- **Related products:** Link to relevant products in the catalogue.

### Enquiry CTA

Use: **Discuss This Product** or **Enquire About Supply**

The button should open the Trade Enquiries page with the selected product prefilled.

Do not introduce checkout, cart, payment, or consumer account functionality.

## Page 5 — Trade Enquiries (/trade-enquiries)

This is one of the most commercially important pages. Its design should prioritise clarity, trust, and ease of completion. Do not make the page look like a SaaS lead-generation funnel.

### Hero

- **Eyebrow:** TRADE ENQUIRIES
- **Headline:** Tell us what your business needs.
- **Supporting copy:** "Share your product requirements with SIWI Global Ventures. Our team can then review your enquiry and discuss the relevant details with you."

### Enquiry form

Create a clean, accessible form. Use a single-column layout for the main form, with a supporting information column on larger screens if appropriate.

**Required fields:**
- Full name.
- Company name.
- Business email.
- Country.
- Product of interest.
- Enquiry details.

**Optional fields:**
- Phone number.
- Approximate order quantity.
- Preferred packaging.
- Additional requirements.

Only ask for information that serves a clear business purpose.

### Product selector options

- Onion Powder.
- Garlic Powder.
- Garlic Granules.
- Dehydrated Fried Onions.
- Birista.
- Onion Kibbles.
- Tiles.
- Other Products.
- Multiple Products.
- Not Sure Yet.

Allow users to describe multiple requirements in the enquiry details field.

### Form behaviour

- Validate required fields.
- Display helpful inline error messages.
- Preserve entered information when validation fails.
- Show a clear submission state.
- Prevent accidental duplicate submissions.
- Provide an accessible success message.
- Include a loading state while submitting.
- Ensure keyboard accessibility.
- Make labels visible rather than relying only on placeholders.

**Success message:** "Thank you for contacting SIWI Global Ventures. Your enquiry has been received."

Only display this confirmation after successful submission.

### Backend requirement

Do not simulate a successful enquiry submission when no backend exists. If a backend or email service is available, connect the form to it. If not, clearly identify the required integration and provide a functional alternative, such as a properly prefilled email enquiry. Do not expose API keys or email-service credentials in client-side code.

## Page 9 — Privacy Policy (/privacy-policy)

Create a readable legal-information page using the same typographic system.

Include appropriate sections covering:
- Information submitted through forms.
- How enquiry information is used.
- Communication relating to business enquiries.
- Relevant service providers, if applicable.
- Data retention, where the actual policy is known.
- Data security practices, described accurately.
- Applicable privacy rights.
- Contact details for privacy-related questions.

Do not fabricate legal guarantees, regulatory compliance, certifications, or specific data-retention periods. The final policy must reflect the actual website implementation and applicable law.

Keep the design simple and text-focused. Do not turn every legal paragraph into a card.

## Page 10 — Terms (/terms)

Create a clean legal-information page covering the actual website's intended use and limitations.

Potential topics include:
- Website information.
- Product information accuracy.
- Enquiry submissions.
- Intellectual property.
- External links.
- Limitations and applicable legal terms.

Do not invent contractual commitments or export terms that the company has not approved.

Use an understated editorial layout.
