Premium International Trade & Export Website — Complete Design System and Multi-Page Experience
1. YOUR ROLE
Act as a senior digital art director, brand identity designer, UX architect, typography specialist, and frontend design engineer.
Design a complete, production-quality, multi-page corporate website for SIWI Global Ventures, an India-based international trading and export company specialising in dehydrated vegetable products and other traded goods.
The website must establish credibility with international buyers, importers, distributors, food manufacturers, wholesalers, and business partners.
This is not a generic corporate website.
It must feel like a carefully considered international trading company with a strong identity, professional standards, clear communication, and an understanding of global business.
The visual quality should combine:
- Apple's restrained typography and precise spatial composition.
- The editorial sophistication of premium international brands.
- The credibility and clarity of established B2B companies.
- The precision of modern industrial and logistics design.
- The warmth and material character of the company's existing black, gold, and ivory branding.
The final result should look designed by a human art director, not assembled by an AI website generator.
Avoid predictable templates, excessive cards, repetitive rounded containers, generic gradients, decorative blobs, and the usual AI-generated SaaS aesthetic.
Every page must have its own visual composition while remaining part of one coherent design system.

3. CORE DESIGN PHILOSOPHY
The website should communicate five things almost immediately:
1. Trust: A credible company that international buyers can contact with confidence.
2. Clarity: Visitors can understand what SIWI exports and how to enquire.
3. Quality: Products and business communication are presented professionally.
4. Global reach: The company connects Indian products with international markets.
5. Reliability: The website feels organised, precise, and easy to navigate.
The design must be sophisticated without becoming pretentious.
Avoid the assumption that premium design means adding more visual effects.
Premium design comes from proportion, typography, contrast, image selection, alignment, whitespace, and the confidence to remove unnecessary elements.
The primary principle
Design with fewer elements, but give every element a clear reason to exist.
Do not fill empty spaces merely because they appear empty.
Do not add a card when a heading, paragraph, divider, or well-composed image would communicate the same information more effectively.
Do not make every section visually identical.
Do not force every page into the same hero-heading-image-card layout.
Each page must have a distinct composition appropriate to its purpose.
4. TYPOGRAPHY — APPLE-INSPIRED, NOT GENERIC AI TYPOGRAPHY
Typography is one of the most important aspects of this website.
Use the restrained, precise typographic approach associated with Apple's product and corporate interfaces.
The goal is not to copy Apple's website. The goal is to apply its discipline to a serious international trade brand.
Primary sans-serif typeface
Prioritise the following font stack:

Use appropriate system-font fallbacks on Windows, Android, and other platforms.

Serif typeface
Use a restrained editorial serif for the SIWI wordmark, selected major headings, and occasional brand statements.
Preferred options:
- Cormorant Garamond
- Libre Baskerville
- Georgia as a system fallback
Use the serif sparingly.
The website should not become an old-fashioned luxury hotel website.
The combination should feel contemporary, commercial, and globally relevant.

Typography rules

- Avoid excessive letter spacing in body text.

- Keep body text line length around 55–75 characters where practical.
- Set body line-height around 1.5–1.75.
- Set display-heading line-height around 0.98–1.12, depending on the typeface.
- Use natural sentence case for most headings.
- Use typographic contrast instead of coloured boxes to establish hierarchy.
- Keep headings short and intentional.

5. SPACING — THE MOST IMPORTANT VISUAL UPGRADE
Implement a deliberate spacing system.
The website must feel spacious, precise, and calm.
Do not compress content into dense grids. Do not scatter elements randomly. Use whitespace to establish relationships between elements.
Base spacing system
Use an 8px-based spacing scale:
:root {
  --space-1: 4px;
  --space-2: 8px;
  --space-3: 12px;
  --space-4: 16px;
  --space-5: 24px;
  --space-6: 32px;
  --space-7: 48px;
  --space-8: 64px;
  --space-9: 96px;
  --space-10: 128px;
  --space-11: 160px;
}

Use these values as a coherent system rather than applying them mechanically.
Page margins
Desktop:
- Main content width: approximately 1120–1320px.
- Horizontal page padding: 48–72px on spacious desktop layouts.
- Section spacing: approximately 96–144px where the content warrants it.
Tablet:
- Horizontal padding: 28–40px.
- Section spacing: approximately 72–96px.
Mobile:
- Horizontal padding: 20–24px.
- Section spacing: approximately 56–80px.
Avoid excessively wide text columns.
Use a maximum-width container for long-form text and a separate wider container for imagery, maps, and visual compositions.
Internal spacing
- Keep headings and their supporting paragraphs closely associated.
- Leave sufficient space between different content groups.
- Separate sections with whitespace, subtle rules, changes in image composition, or changes in background tone.
- Keep button padding proportional to its label.
- Do not give every component identical padding.
- Avoid repetitive vertical spacing that makes the page feel mechanically generated.
The whitespace test
Every section should have a clear focal point.
If a section contains a heading, paragraph, image, and CTA, arrange them according to their importance.
Do not place all four inside a white rounded rectangle merely to make them look organised.
Use layout, alignment, and spacing instead.

- A separate card for every statistic.

- Nested cards.
- Excessive pill-shaped labels.
- Dashboard-style layouts on corporate pages.
- Repeated icon-heading-description combinations.
Prefer
- Editorial columns.
- Asymmetric image layouts.
- Fine horizontal rules.
- Large typographic headings.
- Full-width image sections.
- Simple lists.
- Open layouts.
